Warning Signs for HVAC Maintenance

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • Energy bills creeping up without any change in your habits
  • Longer run times to get to the thermostat setting
  • No real sense of how healthy your heating and cooling equipment is
  • Musty air when the fan starts, or a filter that has been in far too long
  • The system sounds different at startup than it did last season
  • Nobody has opened up the system in over a year
  • A failure last year that seemed to come out of nowhere
  • A newer system whose warranty terms expect documented maintenance

The Benefits of Professional HVAC Maintenance

There is a short list of things homeowners can handle: changing filters on time, keeping the outdoor coil clear, leaving registers open, and checking the drain. The rest takes instruments, because the problems that leave you stranded give no visible warning. A capacitor that has lost 20 percent of its rating looks just like a new one. Low charge shows up only in superheat and subcooling readings. Cracked heat exchangers, weak flame signals, marginal pressure switches, and amp draws creeping toward the breaker's limit are all invisible until measured, and all of them cost less to fix before they fail.

With gas equipment, the stakes go beyond cost. Burners, flame proving, venting, and draft should be verified with instruments every year, because when they fail, the result can be combustion gases in your living space, not just a chilly house. That is why our experienced technicians treat safety testing as the center of every fall visit rather than an add-on. Over time, Bentonville HVAC builds a history of your readings, and the trend lines show which parts are wearing and which still have seasons left. Maintenance Tips for HVAC Maintenance

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Book spring and fall visits in the shoulder seasons, before the rush and before the extremes
  • Note new sounds and longer run times; they are data your technician can use
  • Trim plants back from the outdoor unit and give the coil a gentle rinse every spring
  • Resist closing vents in spare rooms; the system is designed with all registers open
  • If you have gas or other fuel-burning equipment, test your CO detectors every month
  • Flush the condensate line every spring so algae cannot build up and clog it
  • Swap the filter every one to three months, the most valuable habit a homeowner can build
